## 2. What Q1 is now answered

- **P1-5 picked the right three, and the taxes it named are gone — verified in current code, not in commit messages.** One-tap play (shared `playItem()` used by card and reader), cost chips at the decision point, persisted resume for audio *and* article (`audio_pos`/`article_pos` in the same `focu:content-consumption:v1` store; Continue labels with remaining time), contextual recovery with plain-language status, auto-refresh, reader first-screen reorder with the cover capped at 160px, un-compiled sources collapsed to one toggle row, and the glance gist. Each maps to a function or CSS rule in the tree today.
- **The removal era is over.** Every remaining tax found by this walk is a *content or placement* failure, not a control to delete: a gist that says nothing (N1), a name that never arrives (N2), a button at the bottom of an unbounded list (N3), links at the wrong end of an article (§3). The next attention returns come from making surviving surfaces say the right thing, not from removing more of them.
- **Archive semantics drift is confirmed and bounded.** `render()`'s archive branch is `isDone(x.id) || isNotForMe(x.id)` — Archive is completions ∪ dismissals, with **no card-level distinction**; the distinction survives in exactly two places: the reader's state line ("Finished · Saved in Archive" vs "Not for me · Saved in Archive", `updateReaderState`) and the export's `status`/`via` fields. Restore (`markUnread`) reverses both identically, so the ambiguity costs the owner nothing operationally — it costs one open-and-check only when he asks *why* an item is in the Archive. Recorded as still-open below, deliberately not a top-3 finding.
- **The marks' cost side is clean even though their effect side leaked.** Per §1, steps 6–7: the acts are 1 tap, quiet, and correctly absent from Archive cards. The F1 attribution leak (fixed wave 16, commit 8fa8412a) was pipeline-side; the reader-side price of the learning loop is exactly two borderless text actions per card, and this walk finds that price honestly charged and worth it.

## 3. The pipeline side of the same morning

What the 05:44 run hands the reader, measured from the current publisher over the 61 production deliveries:

- **Bundle shape:** one payload `{schema_version: 1, items, episodes (audio subset), research_requests}`, written to `assets/episodes.json` and embedded in the page. Measured composition: 9 daily compilations, 31 registered-source items, 21 user-initiated research items; 18 items carry audio; `reading_minutes` present on 61/61. On the measured bundle **0 registered items are uncovered** — every one is inside some compilation — so the briefing tab's resting state is the compilation cards alone, and the collapsed sources row is a sometimes-surface (mid-day or selector-skipped items), not a daily tax.
- **Agenda:** there is no agenda *field*. The agenda lives as article blocks inside the compilation (Nov 16: block 1 "Here's what came in.", block 2 "Four items today:", blocks 3–6 bullets of `Title — Source`). It is reachable only by opening the briefing and reading its first screen — which works, but means the card layer knows nothing of it (see N1).
- **Gist coverage: 59/61 (97%)**, by origin: research 21/21, compilations 9/9 nominal (but see below), registered 29/31. Gist length 15–91 chars, median 58 — inside the ≤90 budget. The two misses are both LinkedIn Feed digests, and they miss *completely*: `make_gist` finds no honest extract (a digest of post bullets yields no synopsis paragraph and no Findings claim), and the same shape leaves `summary` empty too, so those cards render title + price only.
- **The nominal 9/9 compilation gists are the walk's sharpest datum.** Compilations dated Nov 8–9 carry real gists (their leads were prose). Every compilation from **Nov 10 through Nov 16 — seven consecutive days — has gist = summary = "Here's what came in"**: when the agenda opening was adopted (owner's rule, 2026-11-09), the article lead became a formula sentence and the extractive gist pipeline (`editorial()` takes synopsis → claim → lead paragraph; the agenda bullets are none of those) silently degraded on the single most-opened card in the product. The gist is not wrong; it is empty — the card that should answer "what's today about?" in 0.5s answers nothing.
- **Source names never enter the bundle.** Item `source` values in the measured payload: `"Focu"` ×40, `"Your research"` ×21. `publish.py` sets `source: "Focu"` for everything except user-initiated items, and a registered delivery's `content.json` carries no source-name field at all (it has `content_id, origin, podcast_mode, title, source_url, published_at`). The real names ("OpenAI Official", "LinkedIn Feed") exist only inside the briefing article's agenda bullets and section headings. See N2.
- **One discipline note, no finding:** cost chips show a duration only when the publish ran with audio audit (production's command does; `--ios-compatible-audio` forces it). A future non-audited publish would degrade every chip to a bare "▶ Play" with no error anywhere. Worth a line in the promotion checklist, not a wave.

The owner is phone-first (audit's stated pattern); recorded so the promotion decision sees it.

## 5. New findings (at most 3, ranked by attention returned)

### N1 — The daily briefing card cannot say what the briefing contains

Evidence (§3): gist = "Here's what came in" on 7/7 recent compilations; `normalize()` carries `covered_content_ids` into the reader but `appendCard` never renders a count; the agenda exists only as article text. The morning's first and most important card fails both the 0.5s rung (gist) and the 10s rung (contents) — the owner must open the article to learn what the day holds, every day.
Acceptance (line-checkable): (1) a publish test with a briefing-shaped fixture (formula lead + agenda bullets) yields a gist that reflects the contents — it contains the covered-item count or a covered item's title — and is never the bare lead sentence; gist stays extractive, no generated text; (2) a reader behavior test: a compilation card renders its covered count (`N items`) from `covered_content_ids.length`; (3) non-compilation gists and cards are byte-identical to today (wave-4 tests green unmodified).
Attention returned: the daily orientation question answered at the card, ~10–30s/day plus one avoided open on days the owner only listens.

### N2 — Registered-source items reach the reader named "Focu"; the source name exists nowhere in the bundle

Evidence (§3): measured `source` values are "Focu" ×40 / "Your research" ×21; `publish.py` hardcodes the former; delivery `content.json` has no source-name field; names survive only as article prose. "Follow people, not platforms" is the product's stated principle, and its triage surface cannot name a person or a source: an uncovered item behind the collapsed row is judged by title alone, a covered item's byline says "Focu", and a mark on any registered item records `source: "Focu"` into the learning export's human-readable evidence (attribution itself is safe — wave 16 joins on content ids — but the evidence string a human reads is wrong).
Acceptance: (1) publisher passes a delivery's source name through additively when the delivery records one (absent ⇒ `"Focu"`, byte-identical bundles for today's deliveries; publish test); (2) reader behavior test: an uncovered card's meta line and an opened item's byline show the recorded name — `normalize()` already passes `x.source` through, so the reader change may be zero, and the test should prove it; (3) recording the registry source name into `content.json` at delivery time is production-side and lands at promotion, not in this run — the dev-main deliverable is the contract plus fixtures.
Attention returned: source-level judgment restored at the decide step whenever uncovered items exist, and every future mark's evidence names the thing it judges.

### N3 — The reading-summary export sits at the bottom of an unbounded Archive

Evidence (§1 step 7): `#archivefoot` follows `#grid` in the Archive view's DOM; `render()` lists every done-or-dismissed item with no cap or count; the export is the learning loop's *only* transport (Phase 2 design: no background sync, ever, by sovereignty). A control the owner must reach periodically should not cost a scroll whose length grows with his own history — and if exporting feels expensive, the signals stop flowing and the learning loop starves quietly.
Acceptance: (1) a reader behavior test: in Archive view the Share control precedes the first archive card in DOM order (rendered above the grid), with no new control appearing in Briefing or Research views; (2) the exported payload for a given store is byte-identical to today's (existing wave-5/6 export tests green unmodified); (3) the control keeps its current quiet styling — placement changes, weight does not.
Attention returned: ~10–60s and one long scroll per export session, growing avoided cost every week the archive grows; protects the signal supply the whole learning loop depends on.

## 6. Q1 verdict

The audit asked where the reader taxes attention and whether P1-5 picked the right removals. Re-walked against wave-21 reality: **the picks were right and the removals landed; the core morning ritual costs 1 tap and 0 scrolls.** What taxes attention now is not chrome — it is three places where the surviving surfaces fail to say the thing they exist to say (N1–N3), plus two known residuals (Archive state distinction, the reader's two-button audio failure path) that are cheap, bounded, and recorded above with their evidence. Q1 is answered for this run; N1–N3 are proposed to the backlog in this order.
