## 1. The twelve hunt questions, re-run

**Q1 — Reader attention-tax walk. STILL OPEN, and staler.** The audit's counts predate two post-audit waves: wave-10/11 added a "Not for me" and a "Useful" action to every card (the behavior suite's card-control pin was updated 1 → 2 → 3 controls across those waves) plus an export button in the Archive footer. The recount is now owed against wave-11 reality, not wave-3.

**Q2 — Pack staleness / re-verification. TOOLING ANSWERED; CADENCE OPEN.** `focu pack refresh-check` (packs.py `refresh_check` / `refresh_check_bundled`, tests in `tests/test_pack_refresh.py`) re-probes every pack feed against its recorded state and reports rot — dead, malformed, oversized, moved-on — while writing nothing. The Anthropic v1.1.0 refresh is the worked example: three rot cases (malformed feed, 404, over-cap feed) found within hours of v1.0.0's verification. Two residuals: (a) refresh-check is a manual command — no role or schedule owns running it, so detection still depends on someone remembering; (b) its `verification_stale` signal uses the codebase's `STALE_DAYS = 180`, while observed feed breakage happened in *hours* — the feed-health signals carry the real weight, the age signal is close to decorative at 180 days for people packs.

**Q3 — Affiliation rot. STILL OPEN.** Shipped mitigations are real but partial: every pack source carries `affiliation_evidence` and the pack a `verified_at`, and refresh-check ages the verification. But refresh-check probes *feeds only* — a person who changes employers keeps blogging, the feed stays live, and no signal fires. The run's one affiliation catch (Karpathy → Anthropic, which also corrected the OpenAI pack's picture of Weng's departure-and-return) came from cross-pack human research, not from any mechanism. Recommendation stands as the PRD framed it: keep "affiliation as of" visible (already the de facto state) and treat affiliation re-checking as part of the periodic human/agent pack refresh — do not automate it; a fetcher cannot meet the evidence bar.

**Q4 — Cadence vs. value. CLOSED** @ 4522e84b (cadence_warnings in install/update previews and `focu pack show`; dominance ≥50% of a ≥20/mo pack, or ≥20/mo high-volume; warnings only). Verified in tree: `cadence_warnings` at packs.py, tests in `tests/test_pack_cadence_warnings.py`.

**Q5 — Empty-briefing failure surface. STILL OPEN, narrowed.** The *data* now exists in-repo: the ingest stage report carries per-source status/error (pipeline.py ingest report), and Phase 3 surfaces `learned_paused` / `learned_record_error` in the digest result. The reader's status line speaks only when it is the content (load errors, storage failure). **Q6 — Dedupe pressure across packs. CLOSED, re-verified this sweep.** Both halves shipped (preview cross-locator warning @ b3c5a87f; reader `canonical_url` delegation @ 5186ca91). Re-checked at the identity seam: `focu/daily/config.py:82` derives `source_id = stable_id("src", source_url(src))` and `focu/packs.py:376` (`_entry_identity`) derives it from the *same* `identity.source_url(entry)` — one id space for registry and packs, confirmed in code, not just in tests.

**Q7 — Feedback sparsity floors. STILL OPEN, now blocked behind Finding F1.** The floors shipped as named constants (≥10 delivered in 30 days; demotion needs ≥3 negative events and ≥20% of delivered; promotion needs ≥3 owner useful marks). They have never been validated against real data — and per F1, the main real-data path (marks on compiled briefings) currently cannot attribute at all, so a real-data validation run today would mostly measure the attribution leak, not the floors. Order matters: F1 first, then Q7 becomes answerable.

**Q8 — Learning vs. reporter-only boundary. ANSWERED for this run's scope, with named residuals.** The boundary is in code, not just design: inference may only demote (`learn.py` engine), weights clamp to [0.5, 1.5] at the single consumption point (`learned_adjustments`), caps apply via `min(registry, learned)`, registry priority stays the base, a corrupted record fails closed to zero influence, and every applied factor is frozen into the episode record where `focu learn explain` reads it back. Residuals from learning-design §3.4/§6.1 that are *not* built: renewal proposals (deferred deliberately — they need their own accept semantics), the quarterly long-paused-source proposal ("paused 90+ days: restore, keep, or remove?"), and the "at most one accepted demotion step per 90 days" rule, which today holds only implicitly (an active entry suppresses re-proposal of the same kind+target; nothing stops an accept → supersede → re-propose cycle inside 90 days). These are Finding F5.

**Q9 — Import trust. PARTIALLY ANSWERED; one concrete residual (Finding F3).** Shipped: HTTPS-only locators, credentials and tracking parameters rejected at validation, bounded fetch discipline (15s, 2 MB, serial, no retries), preview-first installs, unverified candidates excluded unless explicitly overridden. Not shipped: the liveness fetch follows redirects without re-validating where they land, and nothing refuses non-public addresses — see F3. Also unexamined: whether `validate_pack` bounds a pack's source count at all (a 10,000-source pack is a self-inflicted attention flood even if every source is legitimate); the F3 wave should check and, if absent, bound it.

**Q10 — The one-full-read budget. STILL OPEN, with hard evidence.** `grep "original" experiments/muse-artifact-reader/index.html` returns zero hits, and `normalize()` (index.html:356-358) does not carry the item's origin URL at all — only the local article path. The reader therefore cannot offer the original: the one deliberate full read happens on Focu's rendered copy or nowhere. For research items the article *is* the deliverable, but for registered-source items the original is the ladder's top rung and it is currently unreachable from the surface that manages all the other rungs. This is Finding F4.

**Q11 — Cold start for a stranger. STILL OPEN, untouched.** Nothing in the run changed onboarding: README still describes six questions and hand-pasted starter packs. The Source Library now makes a smaller first run *possible* (browse → preview → install is one command), but nobody has designed the first-run itself or answered the PRD's question — whose objective does a default starter pack serve? Park as a design item; it matters at promotion-to-main time, not before.

**Q12 — Podcast vs. reader consumption-state divergence. ANSWERED within the reader; residual is production-side.** By construction there is one store: the reader's localStorage consumption store keys every state (audio position, article position, completion) by item id, with `via` distinguishing audio / article / device_voice / manual, and the Phase-2 export preserves `via` end-to-end into ingest (manual archives stay excluded from consumption evidence). Where the surfaces can still disagree is outside the dev surface: an MP3 delivered as a chat attachment and played there writes nothing back to the reader's store. That is a production delivery habit, off-limits this run — recorded here so the promotion decision sees it, not as a dev-main defect.

## 3. New findings, ranked by attention returned

### F1 — Reader marks on compiled briefings can never be attributed to a source; the learning loop's main signal path leaks into `unattributed_feedback`

The chain, each link verified in code: publish.py:86 sets a daily compilation item's `source` to `"Focu"` and the reader keys it by the compilation's `content_id`; the reader *has* the unit-level ids — `normalize()` (index.html:358) carries `covered_content_ids` into every compilation item — but `buildReadingSummary()` emits a fixed per-item key set that omits them. `focu learn ingest` therefore records `observation` events whose `target_id` is the compilation id (learn.py:894-895), and `_attribute_feedback` (learn.py:360-361) joins observations only through `content_source`, a map of *unit* content ids from frozen episode items. A compilation id is never a unit id, so every Useful / Not-for-me mark on a daily briefing — the owner's primary consumption surface — lands in `unattributed_feedback` and can never generate a proposal. Marks on research items (intent ids, held in the research store the statistics never read) are unattributable too; that exclusion is arguably correct, but today the two cases are indistinguishable in the output, so the leak is invisible as well as real. Phase 2 is closed in form and open in fact until this is fixed.
Acceptance (line-checkable): (1) the reading-summary export includes `covered_content_ids` on compilation items, pinned by a reader behavior test; format stays v1-additive per the wave-11 precedent; (2) an ingest fixture whose export marks a compilation `not_for_me` produces attributed negative evidence for its covered sources (fan-out to per-unit observations, or an explicitly reported compilation bucket the engine counts — implementer's choice, stated in the report), and the fixture's `unattributed_feedback` is 0; (3) the ingest report counts research-item marks separately as out-of-scope, not as unattributed; (4) full suite green at ≥469 tests.

### F2 — Three of four adapter prototypes cannot be registered, so their units can never enter a briefing

`KNOWN_TYPES = {"rss", "blog", "youtube", "x", "linkedin"}` (focu/daily/config.py:10); `load_config` raises on any other type, and ingest (pipeline.py:84-86) refuses a unit whose source matches no enabled configured source. The YouTube adapter works (type `youtube` is known) and LinkedIn is the production pattern, but there is no registrable home for an Instagram, RedNote, or Facebook source: `identity.source_url`'s fall-through branch would already serve them via `profile_url` — only the type whitelist blocks. The adapter docstrings admit this ("the config validator must learn the type — a production change not made"), but the backlog marks all four adapters simply done, which overstates what "done" means: three prototypes emit units into a void. The fix is deliberately small because the hard parts (caps, stop conditions, identity) are already built.
Acceptance: (1) `load_config` accepts `type: instagram | rednote | facebook` with a `profile_url` and derives the same `source_id` the adapter's per-day unit identity assumes (test); (2) an ingest test feeds a fixture adapter unit for a registered adapter source and it persists under the registry `source_id`; (3) a unit whose name matches no configured source still raises (existing anti-spoof guard intact); (4) suite green.

### F3 — Pack liveness fetching follows redirects blind and refuses no non-public address (Q9 residual)

`_bounded_fetch_prefix` (packs.py:426-429) is a plain `urlopen`: redirects are followed silently, the final URL is returned but never re-validated, and a grep for `ipaddress` / private-address handling across packs.py and source_collection.py returns nothing. The validator's HTTPS-only rule covers the pack's *entry* URL; it says nothing about where a redirect from that URL lands. For the owner's own packs this is theoretical. For the third-party packs Q9 is about — the moment packs circulate — a feed URL (or a compromised feed host redirecting) can make the owner's machine fetch internal addresses during validate / install / refresh-check. Bounded size and timeout limit the blast radius; they don't address the target.
Acceptance: (1) a direct `feed_url` resolving to a non-public address is rejected at validate time with a named error (test with a literal non-public URL — no live network); (2) a redirect chain whose target is non-HTTPS or non-public is refused in the liveness path and reported as a liveness failure with that reason (test via the injectable fetch/opener); (3) the wave also checks whether `validate_pack` bounds pack source count, and if not, adds a stated bound with a test; (4) existing liveness/refresh tests unchanged-green.

### F4 — The reader has no door to the original: the ladder's top rung is unreachable

Evidence under Q10: zero occurrences of "original" in the reader, and `normalize()` drops the origin URL entirely. Every other rung of the compression ladder is now served — gist for 0.5s, summary for 10s, clean article view for the read — except the last: when an item earns a full read of the *original* (the article's own success case: "perhaps one makes me stop and read the original"), the user must leave and find it themselves. For registered-source items the pipeline knows the URL (it is the unit's canonical URL); it simply never reaches the reader item.
Acceptance: (1) publish emits the origin URL on registered-source items as an additive field (absent ⇒ rendering byte-identical for existing bundles, same discipline as the gist field); (2) the article view shows exactly one quiet "Original" link for items that carry one (behavior test), and none for research items; (3) no new control appears on cards — the door lives at the point of the full read, not in triage; (4) reader + publish suites green.

### F5 — The learning design's anti-spiral machinery is half-built: renewal and long-pause proposals don't exist, and the 90-day step rule is only implicit

learning-design §3.4 promises renewal proposals past `review_after` and §6.1 promises a quarterly "paused 90+ days: restore, keep, or remove?" proposal plus at most one accepted demotion step per source per 90 days. Shipped reality: `review_after` is stored on every entry; the engine generates only `source_weight` proposals; renewal was deferred (recorded in STATE.md) and the quarterly pause proposal was never scheduled anywhere. The 90-day step rule holds only while an entry stays active — supersede it and the same kind+target can be re-proposed and re-accepted inside the window. Without the renewal path, Phase 3's influence is effectively immortal: a weight accepted in November governs March with no scheduled moment where the system itself asks "still true?" — the exact failure mode §6.1 was written against ("stale preferences ruling forever").
Acceptance: (1) `generate_proposals` emits a renewal proposal for an active entry past its `review_after`, carrying current evidence (delivered/completion since the entry was created), within the existing ≤3-proposal budget (test); (2) a source paused >90 days yields at most one restore/keep/remove proposal per quarter (test); (3) re-proposal of a kind+target whose last accepted step is <90 days old is suppressed even when the earlier entry was superseded (test); (4) `focu learn show` prints each active entry's age in days (test); (5) renewal acceptance semantics (refresh `review_after` / supersede, never duplicate-append) are stated in the code and exercised by the renewal test.
